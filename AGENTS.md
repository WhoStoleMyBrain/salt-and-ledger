# Repository guidance

Salt & Ledger is a Unity 6 maritime economic strategy project. The regional foundation now includes Task 1.6's commerce and private industry: six canonical goods, company accounts, finite factory storage, local procurement/sales, physical transfers, and an optional supply challenge in the map-first Unity host. Legacy single-location, two-port, TradingLab, and Task 1.5 scenario contracts remain available. Read `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`, and `docs/MECHANICS.md` before changing its boundaries or behavior.

- Keep `src/SaltAndLedger.Simulation` independent of Unity, presentation, file-system policy, and documentation generation. Preserve `netstandard2.1` and C# 8 compatibility.
- Prefer small concrete types and explicit state transitions. Do not add speculative service layers, plugin systems, or interfaces for deferred features.
- Keep content in `data/content.json`; keep its structural contract in `data/content.schema.json`. Stable IDs are globally unique and must not depend on display names. Keep Python and C# semantic validation aligned.
- Use finite nonnegative integer inventory quantities and checked arithmetic. A failed production command must leave inventory unchanged. Preserve deterministic behavior; do not introduce wall-clock time or unseeded randomness into the simulation.
- Keep world operations atomic: exceptional failure must preserve all inventories, cash balances, controls, and time, including earlier staged attempts. Runtime instances reference immutable canonical definitions. Legacy steps validate commands first, apply controls, then trades in declared order, then production by ascending priority and ordinal instance ID. Expected stock/funds shortages are completed outcomes; do not retain unbounded history inside the world.
- Use nonnegative `long` cash and positive `long` prices with checked public numeric bounds. Trading conserves goods and combined cash. The existing location inventory is market stock shared with production; trader stock stays separate. Preserve the fixed-price baseline and keep experimental pricing parameters in runtime configuration. Only explicit extraction creates goods; consumption is a physical sink with no household payment. Never replenish cash or silently repair stock.
- Preserve the single-location API. `MaritimeWorldState` owns exactly two independent ports, one trader cash account, one ship cargo inventory, and one global tick; never synchronize separate `WorldState` instances. Reuse the shared trade calculator and existing pricing/production operations. Cargo is the maritime trader's only goods inventory; all items occupy one capacity unit.
- Legacy `MaritimeWorldState` steps validate first, then apply controls, docked trades in declared order, departure, production by ordinal port ID and existing building order, and one travel advance before a single atomic commit. Its departure consumes one travel tick; arrival allows trading on the next step. Remote previews are hypothetical, never execution permission. Capacity shortage is ordinary rejection; illegal ship/port actions are exceptional and roll back every port, account, control, ship state, and tick.
- Keep route policies immutable and evaluation stateless over committed snapshots. The legacy maritime path retains one automated trade at most per tick and host-owned enablement. Regional actors store their immutable policy and enablement in authoritative snapshots; run at most four dockside actions per atomic chain, with a fresh evaluation/preview after each staged action. Preserve per-order expenditure caps, reserves, unsold cargo, explicit wait/pause reasons, and no mixed manual commands for an enabled actor.
- Keep `RegionalWorldState` as one owner of all ports, company accounts, ship cargo, private stock, schedules, challenge progress, day, and revision. Instant commands never advance the day; changed commits increment revision and no-op/rejected commands retain snapshot identity. Reject stale decisions/previews within the same day. Daily order is actor priority/ordinal ID, private procurement, public production by port/priority/ID, private production, private sales, consumption by port/rule ID, then voyages and challenge evaluation; exceptional failure rolls back everything. Departure spends no day; committed arrival permits immediate dockside trading. Legacy timing must not change.
- Keep `EnterpriseScenario` shared by Unity and new headless experiments; preserve `ArchipelagoScenario` and `LegacyContent.Timber` for historical regression fixtures. Route distances/speeds are integers, independent of map rendering. Extraction and transformation are explicit canonical recipe kinds; intervals, stock ceilings, and consumption belong to runtime configuration. Shortages/disabled/output-limited facilities remain due without catch-up batches; toggles preserve deadlines. Record source, transformation, sink, and transport flows separately.
- Company cash is authoritative; ship cash is a compatibility projection and must not be counted twice. Explicit company configuration requires zero ship InitialCash. Private factory stock never aliases public stock. Construction pays the local market, starts empty, and schedules the first batch one interval later. Ship/storage transfers require matching ownership and the actual dock, respect both capacities, and move no cash or time. Remote management may trade locally but cannot move goods between ports.
- Factory recipes remain fixed canonical references. Procurement/sales policies use shared exact pricing, marginal limits, reserves, and bounded quantity searches, with at most one transaction per configured good per daily phase. Do not fabricate cost basis or label sale revenue/unsold output as profit. The challenge counts signed net target-market sales minus purchases for its owner, evaluates after atomic operations, and freezes economic mutation on success/failure; sandbox remains unlimited.
- Price dynamic trades on reversible inventory edges with exact arithmetic and mathematical floor. Preserve same-edge `Ask >= Bid`, bounded positive prices, no profitable closed inventory cycles within one unchanged local pricing model without intervening production, and exact uninterrupted order-splitting totals. Cross-port transport profit is permitted. Preview and execution must share calculations; derive snapshot prices from stock, never a mutable price cache. Do not loop over requested units or distinct prices. Keep `UnitPrice` compatibility semantics explicit: dynamic bulk totals may differ from quantity times first marginal price.
- Document non-data-derived behavior in `docs/MECHANICS.md`. Label design proposals as hypotheses or deferred ideas rather than accepted rules.
- Do not hand-edit `docs/reference/` or `generated/encyclopedia.json`. Regenerate and commit those files with content changes.
- Regenerate `docs/experiments/pricing-results.json` with the `--pricing` demo after relevant experiment/calculation changes; use `--pricing --check` to verify it. Keep authored analysis aligned with measured output and distinguish pricing hypotheses from accounting contracts.
- Regenerate `docs/experiments/archipelago-results.json` with `--archipelago` after scenario or regional behavior changes; verify with `--archipelago --check`. Preserve deterministic reports without machine paths/timestamps, full cash/flow reconciliation, and explicit closed-profit versus held-cargo accounting.
- Generate the Task 1.6 experiment with `--enterprise`; verify with `--enterprise --check`. Preserve historical report bytes when changing only the expanded scenario. Reconcile every company once, markets, ship cargo, private storage, construction transfers, extraction, transformations, and consumption. Choose challenge thresholds from measured merchant and industrial/hybrid trials.
- The demo and headless tests use .NET 10; `global.json` selects a stable 10.0 SDK. Keep NuGet lock files and Unity's package manifest/lock synchronized with intentional dependency changes.
- Run `python tools/prepare_unity.py` after simulation or content changes. It rebuilds and stages the simulation DLL and canonical JSON under ignored `unity/Assets/Generated/`; never copy simulation source or another Newtonsoft assembly into Unity. Unity's pinned UPM package supplies JSON support.
- Keep TradingLab a UI Toolkit host over the staged simulation DLL. Preserve Bootstrap. Use snapshots, previews, and structured results; never duplicate economic calculations or movement/time rules. Selection, policy editing/application, and enablement are passive. Explicit actions alone step the world; keep activity and multi-tick execution bounded, and distinguish cash changes from closed empty-cargo gross profit. Document controls and recovery checks in `docs/PLAYTEST.md`.
- Keep ArchipelagoLab separate from TradingLab. Its main-thread presentation clock requests whole regional days with bounded catch-up; speed and interpolation cannot change economic calculations. Selection/panel edits are passive. Opening trade/manual orders pauses playback; manual commands require player automation disabled. Derive map positions, NPC visibility, and dock counts from snapshots, and keep recent activity bounded.
- The contextual trade modal shows exact totals and display-only execution averages, uses authoritative maximum quantities and snapshot-bound execution, blocks map input, and supports local/remote/NPC-read-only modes and Escape/focus recovery. Industry/warehouse UI must distinguish public trade from private transfer. Verify both 1366×768 and 1920×1080 in the actual Editor.
- C#: PascalCase for public types/members, camelCase for local variables/parameters, four-space indentation. Python: snake_case, four-space indentation, standard-library solutions when sufficient. Follow adjacent code.
- Add focused tests for meaningful behavior changes and failure cases. Do not scaffold later roadmap features as part of foundation maintenance.
- Do not claim Unity integration was tested unless the Editor and relevant player build were actually exercised. Use `docs/UNITY.md` for local verification.
