# Salt & Ledger — Task 2.4: Economic Circulation, Municipal Budgets & Price Sanity

**Status:** proposed sequential Codex milestone; **depends on Tasks 2.2 and 2.3 being merged and verified**. Read `AGENTS.md`, `docs/LIVING_CITIES.md`, `docs/EXPANDING_ECONOMY.md`, `docs/EXPANDING_ECONOMY_EXPERIMENTS.md`, `docs/ECONOMY.md`, `docs/GRAND_COMMISSION.md` and actual merged sim/AI-accounting source. This is a **measured tuning and one modest fiscal-mechanics** milestone, not a new monetary/loan system.

## Goal and why this exists

The user completed a genuine v3 Grand Commission on day 140 (slot-3 fixture; seed `-734282136`, Industrial charter, A1, **Coverage pricing**). All-account money reconciles exactly to 11,450 coins, including the outside trading authority; there was **zero mint issuance and zero external-contract inflow**. NPC merchant companies accumulated **3,197.9348** coins from 1,200 initially (about **31.2%** of the 10,250 regional total, versus 11.7% at start). Player cash dropped from 1,200 to **109.0006**. Southhaven/Westhaven markets hold **9.3394/9.6449** coins, respectively, while households hold **1,223.3754/505.325**. Three markets have zero Bread on day 140, while Eastport holds 20.75 Bread and remains prosperous. Production is limited by material flow, retail affordability, market payroll and price floors in different places. **Do not misdiagnose this as currency destruction or universal household insolvency.**

The same save records Northport/Southhaven/Westhaven Furniture pricing dynamic `TargetStock=1` raw quantity subunit (0.0001 goods), despite stocks of 8/1.75/1.0 respectively. This arises under Coverage when luxury demand is disabled or constrained; price formation can hit the minimum of 1 coin even though the reference values are much higher. Investigate actual live executable buy/sell quotes under these settings before modifying formulas.

The requested outcome is an economic system where profitable trade still matters, monetary and material scarcity remain meaningful, **some funds realistically recirculate to local markets and residents** via genuine expenditures or limited fiscal transfers, and price differences reflect comprehensible stock/need relationships. Never hard-cap an NPC balance, silently redistribute 'excess' money, supply free wages, refill stock or auto-mint coins. The same basic rules should apply to player and NPC merchants wherever possible.

## B. Real port-service costs and commercial recirculation

1. Implement a small, documented set of actual **port handling/service fees** (e.g. charged on completed company trade or loading/port use, scaled reasonably by real cargo/transaction value) and/or a modest, positive-realized-margin merchant duty. **Pay from real player/NPC cash** into the actual local public-market or household/treasury account as appropriate; record who paid, who received, port, amount, source activity and time. No hidden sink or grant. Let the simulation determine whether a fee can execute; insufficient funds must create a transparent rejection/wait rather than negative balances or cargo loss.
2. Apply the same fee semantics to player and NPC trade, without a privileged exemption or arbitrary percentage cap on an NPC's existing balance. Taxes on **completed positive economic proceeds** are an acceptable progressive *soft check on accumulation*, but do not retroactively confiscate balances merely because the company crosses a share-of-world-capital threshold. Preserve small trader viability; avoid turning vital NPC transport into permanent fee-induced deadlock.
3. Add to finance/ledger: cumulative fees paid, fees received by each local account, and modeled operating surplus after those expenses. FIFO paid material basis, construction capital, wage expense and public Mint receipts must not be silently reclassified or double counted. No double charge on a partial action, revisit after load, preview or rejected trade.

## C. Modest municipal budget stabilization

1. Implement **one clear, conservative, periodic** household↔municipal fiscal rule, explicitly visible in town accounts: for example, a bounded levy on **household cash above a real next-food affordability reserve**, only when public funds are too low to cover a small known wage/procurement need; and/or bounded public food relief when households are short of their next essential opportunity and the market can fund it after protecting actual payroll. Keep explicit priorities, payment caps, schedule, safeguards against oscillation, and audited events. Never simply equalize the two accounts.
2. All movements are real transfers, not money creation. A town with no food and no productive use for funds should not necessarily recover merely because cash moved from one account to another. A city with zero public cash but wealthy households should have a plausible, limited way to fund essential local employment if inputs and output space permit it. Relief without physical Bread may be ineffective; record this honestly.
3. All public/NPC/player financing must reconcile under the current two-boundary money invariant (Mint issuance and bounded outside commerce remain as before). Cap arithmetic, event history and computational work. Define deterministic daily phase order and document how saved due dates behave.

## D. Price and initial-inventory sanity, with transparent choices

1. Correct the **pathological Coverage luxury target of one raw physical subunit** when no Furniture household demand is currently eligible, via a documented authored price-target floor or fall back to the reasonable reference target. Keep a valid price response to actual stock and paid demand. Show exact *buy vs sell*, target stock, reference price, inventory and why pricing differs across towns. Do not set every town to a uniform Furniture price.
2. Initial role-based stock buffers: ensure plausible **short-lived essential basics**, including Bread, are visible in every generated modern town at start, and limited role-appropriate advanced imported inputs where justified. Preserve role specialization and inevitable trade dependency. Do not add repeating hidden stock deliveries or alter canonical recipes.
3. These economic rules change play outcomes. Add an **explicit economic-rules revision** for newly started modern v3 games rather than silently changing accepted legacy-v3 reproducible seeds, challenge outcomes or saved runs. Old schema-1/2 and earlier modern storage/itinerary rules load unchanged. If the project already has a modern rules version, extend it minimally, don't proliferate needless flags. Give new runs the revised rule set by default while leaving historical identities intact. Update signatures, player help and current-value previews.
4. Preserve exact rational marginal price calculations and bounded execution, floor/ceiling arithmetic, split-order invariance and authoritative preview/execution agreement, including fractional household consumption and warehouse pricing. Preserve deterministic seeding of roles/routes regardless of the new fiscal rule.

## E. No false claims about balance

Do not invent an answer to 'sustained forever'. The measured 3-year Task 2.0 worlds mostly exhaust paid food under prescribed fixed policies. A plausible fiscal rule may help, but it will not solve missing Flour, warehouses full of the wrong goods or a policy that doesn't transport food. The acceptance goal is **understandable, audited mechanisms and measurable incremental improvement in real cases**, not universal prosperity or guaranteed all-seed victory.

Required verification:
