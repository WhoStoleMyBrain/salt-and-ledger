# Game design

Salt & Ledger is a maritime economic strategy game about choosing commitments and understanding their consequences in a finite regional economy. The current run begins with the whole seeded economic map, then four mandatory Grand Commission choices. Sandbox offers the same economy without a deadline.

Meaningful choices connect capital, real inputs, wages, transport time, household purchasing power and sale opportunities. A cheap good in one town is not automatically useful elsewhere: ships need capacity, destinations need money, and factories need complete batches and payroll. Automation executes a bounded authored itinerary; it does not grant profit, free materials or superior prices.

Complementary town roles encourage physical trade and industry. Multiple facilities and up to three ships let a company expand, while local shared stock reduces pointless within-town transfers. Company Value, cash, material margin, wage expense and public development remain distinct so apparent prosperity can be inspected honestly.

The map-first command deck keeps relevant actions nearby. Harbor, Industry, Fleet, Economy and Objectives expose authoritative values and actionable blockers. Inspection, menus and drafts are passive. Readability and clear controls take precedence over decorative density; generated help is shared with the website.

Grand Commission requires all four chosen conditions, not a compensating score. Current welfare and signed net sales can reverse; actual visits and funded production remain cumulative. Exact retry and local continuation support learning without hidden economic repairs. Current long-horizon evidence includes food/payroll/transport failures; sustainability is a research question, not a promised equilibrium.

Historical prototypes are retired. Future characters, independent charter drafts, curses/blessings, more commodities and larger worlds remain [proposals](ROADMAP.md), not placeholder systems or reasons to introduce speculative architecture now.
